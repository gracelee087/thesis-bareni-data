# Study E Model Selection Chronology

Written 2026-09-06. The planning, execution, and author-answer records from September 4–6 were cross-checked. The order below is the order in which the main tests and decisions proceeded. Some work, such as the follow-up Researcher tests and the input preparation for Critic and Curator, ran in parallel; it was not a strictly linear procedure in which the next task started only after all work had finished. The questions are summaries of the actual role inputs, not verbatim quotations. This is a formative model-selection history, not the AsPredicted registration text for the final experiment.

### Who made what?

- **10 normal source texts:** No. 05 records the principle that the author writes them directly without generative AI, and the status that writing and preliminary approval were complete. This is the writing method recorded in the document; the actual writing process was not independently verified, e.g. through typing history. These normal source texts are not lumped together with the AI-tool-generated materials.
- **20 Evaluator defect variants:** Created as variants on the basis of the No. 05 source texts and a predefined defect allocation. Titles were kept, and the intended defects and the original texts were kept private. [No. 07 variant record](../private/07_DEFECT_VARIANTS_PRIVATE.md). The construction intent is distinct from the reference judgments the author scored later.
- **The Gate's new texts, the Writer test's plans and evidence, the Optimizer test's original drafts and fixed critiques, the Planner/Researcher test's fictional materials, lessons, and claims, and the Critic/Curator test's drafts, findings, incidents, and ledgers:** The preparation records state explicitly that these are **AI-synthesised materials generated with an AI tool**. They are not materials written by a separate paid candidate API. `0 new API calls` does not mean `not written by AI`.
- **Reference answers:** For Planner/Researcher and Critic/Curator, the AI tool first **proposed** the acceptable answers and evidence sets, and the author reviewed and revised them. By contrast, the human scores for Evaluator/Gate were written by the author on blank packets. The two kinds of human participation are not described as the same blinded answer-key writing.
- **Candidate model outputs:** Responses of the actual candidate APIs that received the above materials and role instructions. The supplied answer keys, construction intent, and author scores were kept separate from the neutral input to the models being scored. Information needed for the task itself, such as rationale text explaining a judgment or fixed critiques, was included in the role input.
- **Exact authoring model of the AI tool:** Some preparation receipts state explicitly that the internal authoring model ID was not independently recorded. A specific model version of the AI tool is not guessed. The executed candidate API model IDs are distinguished from the AI tool used for authoring.

## 1. First of all — building the human reference for testing the independent evaluator

- **Why first:** The tool for later evaluating the Writer's and Optimizer's texts and H3 had to be prepared first.
- **Materials:** 10 Bareni normal source texts, each with one subtle-defect and one critical-defect version (30 texts in total). The normal source texts were prepared under the principle of direct authorship by the author. They are evaluated within the supplied company and task evidence and are not real customer data.
- **Criteria/examples for creating the texts:** The normal titles, bodies, task instructions, fact-checking materials, and the frozen defect allocation from No. 05 were used. No. 07 retains a variant that adds to the task-selection explanation of a normal source text the unsupported result "customers reduced their weekly management time by 40%". The 40% is not a figure taken from real statistics but a **test claim inserted for fabrication detection**. It is not separate independent customer evidence.
- **Author's work:** Before seeing the model results, the author scored the 30 texts on audience fit (PF), clarity (CL), actionability (AC), and factual grounding (FG), each 1–5, plus whether critical and the reason. The author then immediately re-scored 10 with new IDs and order. The 10/10 judgment agreement is one author's immediate repeat consistency, not external human agreement.
- **Top-priority criterion:** Not wrongly passing low-quality texts, especially not missing critical defects. A text passes only if all four dimensions are 3 or higher and there is no critical violation.
- Evidence: No. 04 (`04_EVALUATOR_SELECTION_PROTOCOL.md`, Korean-language record; not linked), No. 05 (`05_NORMAL_SOURCE_TEXT_TEMPLATE.md`, Korean-language record; not linked), No. 10 (`10_AUTHOR_RATING_ROUND1_RAW.md`, Korean-language record; not linked), No. 11 (`11_AUTHOR_RATING_RERATE_RAW.md`, Korean-language record; not linked), [No. 12](../12_AUTHOR_REFERENCE_VALIDATION_RESULTS.md).

## 2. First paid LLM comparison — Evaluator selection

- **Question given to the models:** "Score this text on the four dimensions using the supplied evidence, persona, and rubric, and output critical violations and whether it passes." The same 30 texts were evaluated for each candidate.
- **First candidates:** GPT-5.6 Luna, Haiku4.5, Gemini3.5 Flash-Lite. In the first run nothing was selected because of API schema and semantic validation failures. In v2, with the request/output contract fixed, Luna was selected.
- **Reason for the next comparison:** A rerun of the 90 evaluations was conducted with a candidate group of similar price. Mini, Haiku, and Gemini3.8 Flash were frozen separately as the v3 candidate group. This reused the existing 30 texts and is not a new independent validation.
- **Measurement/results:** Only candidates that validly evaluated all 10 human-reference critical texts and missed none were eligible. They were then compared in the order FN→FP→failures→cost→latency. In v3, Gemini3.8 Flash was selected, with author-judgment agreement 25/30, critical 10/10, and 5 subtle defects missed. This was not a test that demonstrated superiority over Luna.
- Evidence: [No. 14](../14_EVALUATOR_SELECTION_RESULTS.md), [No. 16](../16_EVALUATOR_RERUN_V2_RESULTS.md), No. 19 (`19_EVALUATOR_PRICE_MATCHED_PROTOCOL.md`, Korean-language record; not linked), [No. 21](L05_evaluator_selection.md).

## Common measurement method and the scope of consistency

The core flow was: review of materials and acceptable answers → synthetic API-format check → freezing of the exact inputs, models, and criteria → candidate outputs → mechanical checks of format/ID/verbatim quotation → human content audit → candidate mapping/aggregation after the audit was preserved. The retry and stopping rules of the early Evaluator and of the later roles were not identical; each followed the rules of its own run document. We do not write that every stage was fully blinded.

**What the author did:** The normal-source preparation principle, rubric scoring, re-scoring, input/answer-key revisions, semantic audit of candidate outputs, and decisions on execution scope and on adding candidates. **What the AI tool did:** Proposing role items/prompts, preparing neutral packets/model mapping tables, API execution, format validation, preserving originals/hashes/costs, aggregating author scores, and separate review opinions. **What the candidates did:** Generating outputs for their assigned roles. Gemini's text scores did not replace the role audits of Planner, Researcher, Critic, and Curator.

12/24/30 are numbers for a manageable formative-evaluation scale and for covering failure types. They are not a minimum N for a universal performance guarantee, nor numbers derived from H3 power. For the Researcher, a new-item stage was added because the semantic procedure was revised after seeing failures. It is not that an equal number of tests was forcibly added to roles that passed the criteria first time. Candidate expansion was sequential development following earlier results and author decisions, and is not described as a confirmatory comparison that planned all candidates together from the start.
